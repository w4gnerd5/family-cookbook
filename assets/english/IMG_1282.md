# Slow-Roasted Crispy Pork Belly

This recipe utilizes a "confit" technique where the pork belly slow-roasts in its own rendered fat at a low temperature, ensuring incredibly tender meat. The core science for achieving superior crackling involves three specific factors: not scoring the skin to prevent internal moisture from softening the top layer, thorough dehydration of the skin in the refrigerator, and maintaining a perfectly level surface with foil to ensure even heat distribution during the high-heat crisping phase.

## Ingredients

> * 1kg / 2 lb pork belly with skin on, NOT SCORED. Look for 3 - 3.5cm / 1.2 - 1.6" thick, even thickness with flat, unwrinkled skin, preferably not vac-packed (Note 1)
> * 1 1/2 tsp olive oil, divided
> * 1/2 tsp fennel powder / ground fennel seeds, optional, Note 3
> * 1 tsp salt, cooking/kosher salt (or 7/8 tsp table salt), divided
> * 1/4 tsp black pepper
>
> **For serving (as pictured in post):**
> * Apple Sauce
> * Vermouth Jus
> * Cauliflower Puree, (Note 2)
> * See in post for more Sauce options

## Instructions

> 1. **Dry skin overnight:** Place pork belly on a plate. Pat skin dry with paper towels. Leave uncovered in fridge overnight to dry out the skin. (If you don't have time, pat the skin dry with paper towels).

## Notes

Success depends on **NOT scoring the skin**, which prevents juices from bubbling up and softening the crackling. Dehydrating the skin in the fridge overnight is essential for a glass-like crunch, and keeping the pork perfectly level using foil balls prevents fat from pooling in uneven spots during the roasting process.
