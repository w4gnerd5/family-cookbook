# Recipe: No-Knead Bread

This iconic Jim Lahey recipe revolutionized home baking by replacing manual labor with time. A long, slow fermentation at room temperature allows enzymes to develop gluten structure naturally, while baking the high-hydration dough in a preheated heavy pot creates a steam-filled environment that mimics a professional deck oven for a crackly crust and airy crumb.

## Ingredients
> * 3 cups all-purpose or bread flour, more for dusting
> * 1/4 teaspoon instant yeast (Note: Handwritten correction suggests 1/3 teaspoon active dry yeast)
> * 1 1/4 teaspoons salt
> * Cornmeal or wheat bran as needed
> * 1 5/8 cups water

## Instructions
> 1. In a large bowl combine flour, yeast and salt. Add 1 5/8 cups water, and stir until blended; dough will be shaggy and sticky. Cover bowl with plastic wrap. Let dough rest at least 12 hours, preferably about 18, at warm room temperature, about 70 degrees.
> 2. Dough is ready when its surface is dotted with bubbles. Lightly flour a work surface and place dough on it; sprinkle it with a little more flour and fold it over on itself once or twice. Cover loosely with plastic wrap and let rest about 15 minutes.
> 3. Using just enough flour to keep dough from sticking to work surface or to your fingers, gently and quickly shape dough into a ball. Generously coat a cotton towel (not terry cloth) with flour, wheat bran or cornmeal; put dough seam side down on towel and dust with more flour, bran or cornmeal. Cover with another cotton towel and let rise for about 2 hours. When it is ready, dough will be more than double in size and will not readily spring back when poked with a finger.
> 4. At least a half-hour before dough is ready, heat oven to 450 degrees. Put a 6- to 8-quart heavy covered pot (cast iron, enamel, Pyrex or ceramic) in oven as it heats. When dough is ready, carefully remove pot from oven. Slide your hand under towel and turn dough over into pot, seam side up; it may look like a mess, but that is O.K. Shake pan once or twice if dough is unevenly distributed; it will straighten out as it bakes. Cover with lid and bake 30 minutes, then remove lid and bake another 15 to 30 minutes, until loaf is beautifully brown. Cool on a rack.

## Notes
* **Temperature Control:** Maintain a consistent room temperature of approximately 70°F for the long rise; cooler temps will require more time, warmer temps less.
* **Texture Cues:** Ensure the dough is "shaggy and sticky" initially and "dotted with bubbles" before shaping; these are the primary indicators of proper hydration and fermentation.
* **Vessel:** Use a heavy-walled pot (like a Dutch oven) to ensure heat retention and moisture trapping.
