# Crispy Pork and Watermelon Salad

Zak Pelaccio's signature dish from his Fatty Crab restaurants, printed from Serious Eats (from the book "Eat with Your Hands"). Brined, braised, pressed, and deep-fried pork belly tossed with chilled watermelon, pickled watermelon rind, Thai basil, and Vietnamese mint in a palm sugar-lime dressing.

## Ingredients

**For the Watermelon Salad:**
* 2 cups rice vinegar
* 3 shallots, thinly sliced
* 2 fresh Thai bird chilies, thinly sliced
* 2 fresh kaffir lime leaves
* 2 inches fresh ginger, peeled and sliced
* 1 1/2 ounces palm sugar (1 round gula jawa) or 2 tablespoons light brown sugar
* 1 tablespoon kosher salt
* One 5-pound watermelon

**For the Dressing:**
* 3 ounces palm sugar (2 rounds gula jawa) or 1/4 cup light brown sugar
* 1 cup rice vinegar
* 1/2 cup fresh lime juice
* 4 inches fresh ginger, peeled and sliced
* 6 fresh cilantro roots with 1 inch of stems, rinsed, scraped, and rinsed again, or 24 cilantro stems
* 2 garlic cloves, chopped
* 3/4 teaspoon kosher salt

**For the Pork:**
* About 3 cups rendered leaf lard or neutral oil, such as grapeseed or canola
* 2 pounds Braised Pork Belly (recipe follows), cut into 1-inch chunks while still cold
* 1 cup all-purpose flour
* Sea salt

**To finish the dish:**
* 1 cup fresh Vietnamese mint (rau ram) leaves
* 1 cup fresh Thai basil leaves
* 3 scallions, sliced
* 1/4 cup sesame seeds

**Braised Pork Belly, For the Brine:**
* 1/4 cup coriander seeds
* 2 tablespoons fennel seeds
* 4 cups sea salt
* 2 cups sugar
* 10 garlic cloves, crushed and peeled
* 10 fresh long red chilies, such as Anaheim or Hungarian Wax, thinly sliced
* 5 medium shallots, sliced
* 3 tablespoons black peppercorns
* 1 cup packed daun salam (Indonesian bay leaves)
* 2 to 3 pounds fresh pork belly

**For the Braise:**
* 2 tablespoons rendered leaf lard or olive oil
* 1 yellow onion, cut into sixths
* 1 head of garlic, halved crosswise
* 2 shallots, halved lengthwise
* 2 1/2 cups chicken stock

## Instructions

1. Brine the pork belly: Toast the coriander and fennel seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, swirling and shaking until they crackle lightly and release their aroma, about 3 minutes. Set aside.
2. Combine the toasted seeds with the salt, sugar, garlic, chilies, shallots, peppercorns, daun salam, and 3 gallons water in a stockpot and bring to a boil over high heat. Reduce the heat and simmer the brine about 20 minutes. Remove from the heat, cool to room temperature, then refrigerate. When completely cool, pour over the belly and refrigerate, covered, for at least 24 and up to 48 hours.
3. Braise the pork belly: Preheat the oven to 225°F. Cut a piece of parchment paper to fit your roasting pan.
4. Remove the belly from the brine, rinse with cold water, and pat dry. Set aside.
5. In the roasting pan straddling two burners over medium-high, heat the leaf lard until hot but not smoking. Add the onion, garlic, and shallots, and cook, stirring occasionally, until golden and tender, 5 to 6 minutes. Add the stock and deglaze, scraping up any bits with a wooden spoon.
6. When the stock comes to a boil, add the belly to the roasting pan and remove from the heat. Top the pan with the parchment paper and cover with a tight lid or foil.
7. Braise the belly in the oven until tender (a paring knife tip meets no resistance). Start checking after 2 hours; it could take up to 4 hours.
8. Remove the pan from the oven and let it sit at room temperature for 30 minutes.
9. Transfer the belly to a large plate or sheet tray and cool to room temperature. Strain the braising liquid and reserve (fridge up to 5 days, freezer up to 3 months).
10. Wrap the belly tightly in plastic wrap. Press it between two flat pans with a weight on top and refrigerate, pressed, for at least 12 hours and up to 2 days.
11. After cooking and pressing, the belly keeps in the fridge about 2 weeks wrapped tightly, or up to 6 months frozen. Thaw completely in the fridge before use.
12. Make the watermelon salad: Combine everything for the salad except the watermelon in a small saucepan. Add 1 cup water, bring to a boil over medium heat, and cook, stirring, until the sugar dissolves.
13. Meanwhile, cut the rind from the watermelon flesh, reserving both. Pare away the outer dark green skin, leaving the whiter fleshy rind; discard the peel. Cut the white rind into 1/4-inch cubes and put them in a bowl. Strain the seasoned vinegar liquid over the diced rind. Cool, then chill at least 1 hour and up to 2 days.
14. Cut the watermelon flesh into 1-inch pieces, discarding seeds. Chill until ready to use.
15. Make the dressing: Roughly crush the palm sugar with a mortar and pestle or a bag and rolling pin. Pulse the sugar with the vinegar, lime juice, ginger, cilantro roots, garlic, and salt in a food processor until smooth and well combined.
16. Fry the pork: In a large, straight-sided sauté pan or wok, heat 3 inches of lard to 375°F on a deep-frying thermometer.
17. Toss the cubed pork belly in the flour and shake off any excess. Working in batches, fry until very crisp and deep golden brown, 6 to 7 minutes. Drain on a paper-towel-lined tray and season with salt; return the fat to 375°F between batches.
18. Finish the salad: Tear the mint and basil leaves. In a mixing bowl, toss the pickled watermelon rind and chilled watermelon flesh with the scallions, mint, and basil. Add just enough dressing to coat. Divide the pork among plates, top with the salad, and garnish with sesame seeds. Serve immediately.

## Notes

* **Heat:** The Serious Eats notes suggest adding a sliced Thai chile or two if you want more heat; cantaloupe or citrus segments can substitute for watermelon.
* **Yield:** Serves 4 to 6; active time 1 hour, total 1 hour plus one day.

Zak Pelaccio's Crispy Pork and Watermelon Salad Recipe

There's a reason this Crispy Pork and Watermelon Salad is the signature dish at Zakary Pelaccio's Fatty Crab restaurants. It highlights the best of Pelaccio's cooking style, combining his love of Southeast Asian flavors with an irreverent American lean.

Not too many so-called salad recipes begin with slow braising pork belly, but that's kind of the best part of this one. Cool cubes of watermelon and pickled watermelon rind are tossed with deep fried chunks of pork belly, and finished off with torn leaves of Thai basil and Vietnamese mint. It's sweet and summery while having a round, porky richness that makes the salad totally irresistible. Oh, and take this salad outside, add a glass or two of off-dry Riesling and the cool sounds of Coltrane and you really can't go wrong.

Recommended playlist and drinks pairing:
Listen: Coltrane, Blue Train —the whole album; it never gets old.
Drink: Selbach-Oster Riesling Auslese 2005

What Worked: There's a reason this salad has been on the menu at Fatty Crab forever, it's a classic.

What Didn't: Pelaccio isn't shy with the chiles and if you subscribe to the same school of thought you might want to add sliced Thai chile or two to up the heat factor.

Suggested Tweaks: We could see this salad working equally well with mellow cantaloupe or even citrus segments.
