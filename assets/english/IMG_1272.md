# Indian Pudding

Indian Pudding is a traditional New England dessert that evolved from British hasty pudding by substituting cornmeal (maize) for wheat flour, reflecting the adaptation of colonial techniques to indigenous American ingredients. The success of this dish relies on a very slow bake, which allows the cornmeal to fully hydrate and the molasses to caramelize into a deep, custardy consistency.

## Ingredients

> - 1 quart plus 1/2 cup (1 L plus 120 ml) **milk**, divided
> - 1/2 cup (80 g) **cornmeal**
> - 1 teaspoon **salt**
> - 1/2 cup (180 g) **molasses**
> - 1 1/2 teaspoons **ground ginger or cinnamon**
> - **Lightly whipped cream**, for serving

## Instructions

> 1. **Preheat and prepare dish**
>    - Heat oven to **300°F / 150°C**.
>    - Butter the bottom and sides of a **9 x 9 inch** baking dish.
> 2. **Scald the milk**
>    - Place **1 quart (1 L) milk** in a saucepan or double boiler.
>    - Heat to about **180°F / 82°C**, until small bubbles form at the edges.
>    - Stir frequently so it does not burn or form a skin.
> 3. **Mix dry ingredients**
>    - In a bowl, whisk together:
>      - **1/2 cup cornmeal**
>      - **1 teaspoon salt**
> 4. **Cook cornmeal in milk**
>    - When the milk reaches temperature, reduce heat to low.
>    - Slowly whisk in the cornmeal–salt mixture.
>    - Cook for **10 minutes**, stirring constantly.
> 5. **Add molasses**
>    - Stir in **1/2 cup molasses**.
>    - Cook and stir for **2 minutes**.
> 6. **Add spice**
>    - Stir in **1 1/2 teaspoons ginger or cinnamon**.
>    - If using ground ginger, sift it in to avoid clumps.
>    - Cook another **1 minute**, until smooth.
> 7. **Pan and top with cold milk**
>    - Pour the hot batter into the prepared baking dish.
>    - Gently pour **remaining 1/2 cup (120 ml) cold milk** over the top.
>    - **Do not stir it in.**
> 8. **Bake**
>    - Bake for **3 to 4 hours**.
>    - A darker top and a **thin film / skin** are normal.
> 9. **Cool and serve**
>    - Let the pudding cool completely so it firms up **Max 2 hours**.
>    - **Refrigerate:** Cover the baking dish (parchment paper pressed onto the surface can prevent a thick skin from forming, or just leave as is for the traditional "skin") and refrigerate it overnight, or for several hours until completely chilled and set.
>    - Scoop into bowls, breaking through the top as needed.
>    - Top with **lightly whipped cream** and serve.

## Notes

- Ensure the milk reaches the scald temperature (180°F) to prevent a grainy texture in the final pudding.
- It is vital not to stir the final addition of cold milk; this creates the signature custardy top layer.
- Long baking is essential; if the pudding looks too thin at 3 hours, continue baking until it has thickened and darkened.
