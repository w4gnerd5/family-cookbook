# Pirozhki with Scalded Yeast Dough

This recipe utilizes the "scalded dough" (zavarnoy) technique, where boiling water is used to pre-gelatinize a portion of the flour starch. This scientific process creates a uniquely supple and elastic dough that remains soft for several days and is particularly well-suited for deep-frying, providing a superior texture compared to standard yeast doughs.

## Ingredients

**Stage 1:**
* 1 tsp salt
* 1 cup boiling water
* 3 tbsp flour
* 1 tbsp sugar
* 3 tbsp vegetable oil

**Stage 2:**
* 17.5 g yeast
* 1 cup water (boiled, but not hot)
* 500 g flour

## Instructions

**Stage 1:**
1. Combine the flour, oil, sugar, and salt. Pour 1 cup of rolling boiling water over the mixture. Mix thoroughly and allow the mixture to cool.

**Stage 2:**
1. Into the warm mixture, add the yeast and the cup of warm water. Gradually incorporate the flour in parts and knead the dough.
2. Form the dough into small balls and let them rest (spread) for 5 minutes.
3. Shape the pirozhki and fry them in a large amount of oil in a skillet.
4. This yields 20–30 pieces. They can also be baked.

## Notes

* Ensure the "scalded" base from Stage 1 has cooled to a lukewarm temperature before adding the yeast in Stage 2; if the mixture is too hot, it will kill the yeast and the dough will fail to rise. When frying, maintaining a consistent medium-high oil temperature is vital for a golden-brown crust without oil-logging the interior.
