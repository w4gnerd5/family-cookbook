# Khachapuri Recipe by Gia Agirba

Imeretian Khachapuri is a cornerstone of Georgian culinary tradition, celebrated for its perfect balance of salty, crumbly Imeretian cheese and elastic Sulguni. The key to a successful khachapuri lies in achieving a high-hydration, soft dough that is handled gently to preserve its airy structure, ensuring a tender crust that complements the rich, molten cheese filling.

## Ingredients

* Warm Water: 300 ml
* Milk - optional
* Yeast: approx. 5g for 2 portions
* Salt: To taste
* Sugar: 1 teaspoon
* Flour: 450-500 g
* Vegetable oil: 30–40 ml (for kneading)

**Cheese Filling (for 2 Khachapuri):**
* Imeretian cheese: 400 g
* Fresh Sulguni cheese: 200 g
* Eggs: 2 eggs (mixed into the cheese)

## Instructions

1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Add a small amount of milk to the mixture.
2. Combine the liquid with the flour and salt.
3. Knead the dough until smooth. Add 30–40 ml of vegetable oil while kneading.
4. Rub a bit of oil over the surface of the dough and let it rest in a warm place until it rises.
5. Prepare the filling by mixing the crumbled Imeretian cheese, grated Sulguni, and eggs. Each khachapuri should contain approximately 300 g of filling.
6. Once the dough has risen, sprinkle flour on your workspace. Take a portion of dough for one khachapuri.
7. Shape and flatten the dough using circular movements.
8. Place the cheese filling in the center, wrap the dough around it to seal, flatten and let it rest for 10 min
9. Place in oven for roughly 20 - 25 minutes 200C (~425F).

## Notes

* **Temperature Control:** Ensure the water is lukewarm (not hot) to avoid killing the yeast; keep the rising dough in a draft-free, warm spot.
* **Handling:** Use oil on your hands rather than excessive flour to manage stickiness, which keeps the dough soft and light. The dough will take roughly 40 - 45 minutes to rise.
* **Cheese Ratio:** For the best results, use a 2:1 ratio of Imeretian cheese to Sulguni to achieve the traditional flavor profile and texture.
* **Alternative Cheeses:** If Imeretian and Sulguni cheeses are hard to come by, you may use mexican and mazarella cheese as substitutes (respectively).
