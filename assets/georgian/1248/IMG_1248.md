# Satsivi for One Large Turkey

Satsivi is the crown jewel of Georgian festive cuisine, traditionally served cold during Christmas and New Year celebrations. The dish's unique chemistry relies on the high natural oil content of finely ground walnuts and a specific aromatic blend of warming spices—like blue fenugreek and marigold—which emulsify with the broth to create a rich, velvety sauce without the need for additional thickeners.

## Ingredients

> * 1 Large Turkey
> * Onions
> * Walnuts: 600g (finely ground)
> * Dried Coriander: 1 dessert spoon
> * Blue Fenugreek (Utskho Suneli): 1/2 dessert spoon
> * Savory: A small amount
> * Marigold (Yellow Flower): 1/2 dessert spoon
> * Red Pepper: To taste
> * Black Pepper: On the tip of a knife
> * Cloves: On the tip of a knife
> * Cinnamon: On the tip of a knife
> * White Vinegar: 1 tablespoon
> * Eggs: 2 pieces
> * Salt: To taste (for everything)
> * Garlic: 4-5 cloves (processed in a food processor)
> * Vegetable oil
> * Imeretian Saffron ("Yellow flower")

## Instructions

> * Boil the turkey separately.
> * Sauté a substantial amount of finely chopped onions in the fat rendered from the turkey broth. 
> * Do not let the onions brown or burn; they should remain pale and soft.
> * Once the onions are ready, allow them to cool completely.
> * Move them into a blender.
> * While processing, gradually add walnuts and blended herbs to the blender.
> * Afterward, blend everything together witht the broth —determine the thickness by eye.
> * Keep in mind that the mixture will double or tripple in thickness after boiling.
> * Do not bring it to a full boil [too rapidly], otherwise it will curdle like cottage cheese.
> * As soon as it begins to simmer, stir it with a spoon.
> * Continue to stir in this manner until walnuts are boiled through.
> * Season with salt to taste; add more if necessary.
> * Finally, add a little white wine vinegar.
> * Stir, bring it to a boil once more, and then remove it from the heat.
> * Pour the hot sauce over an oven roasted turkey, cut into pieces ahead of a time
> * Cover the pot with the chese cloth, not a lid - otherwise it will "sweat" and will go bad/taste sour

## Notes
* **Onions:** It is easier if the onions are processed in a food processor before sautéing
* **Texture:** For the most authentic result, grind the walnuts until they are as fine as flour to ensure a completely smooth sauce.
* **Onion Prep:** When sautéing the onions in the broth fat, keep the heat low to ensure they soften completely without caramelizing, which would alter the classic pale color of the sauce.
* **Serving:** Satsivi must be prepared in advance and served cold or at room temperature to allow the walnut sauce to thicken naturally and the spices to fully bloom.
* **Preventing Curdling:** To ensure the sauce does not "split" or take on a curdled, cottage-cheese-like texture, avoid a vigorous rolling boil. Maintain gentle heat and stir constantly once the mixture warms.
* **Texture Cues:** The sauce naturally thickens as it cools; the recipe notes it will become twice as thick, so avoid over-reducing it while it is on the stove.
