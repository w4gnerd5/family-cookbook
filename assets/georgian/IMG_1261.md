# Apple Layered Strudel and Easter Paska

These recipes represent quintessential Georgian home baking, focusing on rich, dairy-based doughs and seasonal flavors. The Easter Paska is a celebratory yeast bread that relies on an "Opara" (starter) method to achieve a light, airy texture, a process that requires precise temperature control to ensure the yeast remains active without being scalded.

## Ingredients:

> ### Apple Layered Strudel
> * 200 g Matsoni (Georgian yogurt)
> * 200 g Sour cream
> * 200 g Melted butter
> * 1 cup Sugar
> * 1 tsp Baking soda
> * 3 cups Flour (to be divided into 3 dough balls)
> * 2.5 kg Apples (approx. 5-6 kg raw weight)
> * Sugar (to taste for the filling)
> * Walnuts and Raisins (for sprinkling)
>
> ### Easter Paska
> * 1 L Milk
> * 10 Eggs
> * 1 kg Sugar
> * 5 tsp Yeast (timing may vary)
> * 800 g Melted butter
> * ~ 2.5 kg - 3 kg Flour
> * 400 g Raisins

## Instructions:

> ### For the Apple Strudel
> 1. Mix the matsoni, sour cream, melted butter, sugar, and baking soda.
> 2. Incorporate the flour and divide the resulting dough into 3 equal balls.
> 3. Layer the dough with prepared apples (sweetened to taste) and sprinkle walnuts and raisins between the layers and on top.
>
> ### For the Easter Paska
> 1. **Prepare the Starter (Opara):** In a separate bowl, combine 1 L of warm milk with 2 tablespoons of sugar, a pinch of flour, and the yeast. Wrap the bowl warmly and let it sit until the mixture rises and bubbles. Once risen, perform the first light kneading.
> 2. **Main Mixture:** Melt the butter with the sugar and incorporate egg yolks that have been whisked until pale and white.
> 3. **Combine:** Add spices (previously infused in cognac or spirits) to the butter and egg mixture. 
> 4. **Dough Preparation:** Once the wet ingredients are combined, slowly begin adding the flour. 
> 5. **Kneading:** The dough must be fluffy and should not stick to your hands. If it starts to stick, grease your hands with oil to continue working it.
> 6. **Final Step:** Knead the raisins into the dough at the very end. Cover with a clean towel and allow to rise.

## Notes:

> * **Temperature Control:** The milk used for the yeast starter must be warm, never hot, or it will kill the yeast.
> * **Dough Texture:** The Paska dough should remain soft and "fluffy." Use oil on your hands rather than excessive flour if sticking occurs to maintain the correct consistency.
> * **Spices:** For the best flavor, let your spices sit in cognac or alcohol before adding them to the dough.
