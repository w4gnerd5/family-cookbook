# Puff Pastry and Layered Pirog

This recipe describes a "rough puff" or flaky pastry dough where the key to success is maintaining the cold temperature of the ingredients. By cutting the butter into the flour rather than fully incorporating it, you create distinct layers of fat that release steam during baking, creating the airy, shattered texture essential for traditional Slavic sweet or savory pies.

## Ingredients

**For the Dough:**

* Flour: 250 g
* Cold butter: 400-450 g
* Cold water: 6-8 tablespoons
* Salt: 1/4 teaspoon

**For the Pirog Filling:**

* Cooked rice
* Braised cabbage with onions
* Hard-boiled eggs
* Boiled, finely chopped beef
* Black pepper
* Water or egg white, for sealing
* Egg yolk and a little water, for the wash
* Poppy seeds (optional)

**For the Christmas Ghvezeli:**

* Cheese
* Hard-boiled eggs
* Melted butter
* A button or a coin

## Instructions

**Making the Dough:**

1. In a large bowl, mix the flour and salt.
2. Cut the chilled butter into cubes, working directly in the bowl with the flour.
3. Continue cutting until the cubes are roughly 0.5 cm.
4. Add a little cold water and stir with a fork; if the dough is still crumbly, add a little more.
5. Work the dough with your hands into a ball. Do not knead much, or the butter will melt.
6. Wrap in plastic and chill: 1 hour in the refrigerator, or 15-20 minutes in the freezer.

**Laminating the Dough:**

1. Take the dough out of the refrigerator.
2. Dust with flour and roll into a strip about 5 mm thick.
3. Fold the strip in four along its length: bring both ends into the centre, then fold the whole piece in half. This step is what makes the dough rise.
4. Roll it out into a strip again and fold it the same way.
5. Wrap in plastic and chill again, 1 hour in the refrigerator or 15-20 minutes in the freezer.
6. Repeat the whole cycle twice more, so the pastry comes out light.

**Assembling the Pirog:**

1. Divide the dough in two.
2. Roll the chilled dough to 3 mm and transfer it to a baking sheet.
3. Layer the filling: rice first, then the braised cabbage and onion.
4. Top with hard-boiled eggs, cut in halves or quarters lengthwise.
5. Add the boiled, finely chopped beef, and season with black pepper.
6. Wet the edges of the dough with water or egg white.
7. Roll out the second half of the dough and lay it over the top.
8. Press the edges together with your fingers to seal, and trim the excess.
9. Run the back of a fork along the edge for a pattern.
10. Brush with egg yolk beaten with a little water.
11. Sprinkle with poppy seeds if you like, and prick the top in several places.
12. Bake at 220°C (430°F) for 35 minutes, until well browned on top.

**Baking, and the Christmas Ghvezeli:**

1. Start with a cool oven. If it is hot at the beginning the pastry will not rise.
2. Make three holes in the baked pie and pour melted butter into them.
3. The same dough makes a Christmas *ghvezeli* filled with cheese and boiled eggs, the eggs cut in half.
4. Put a button or a coin inside and make a wish. Shape it the same way, only without the holes and the butter.
5. Mushrooms and tarragon can stand in for the cabbage.

## Notes

* **Temperature is everything:** use ice-cold water and butter straight from the refrigerator. If the dough turns sticky or elastic while rolling, put it back in the freezer for ten minutes before going on. Butter that melts into the flour gives a greasy pastry instead of a flaky one.
* **The cold oven:** the layers need to separate and expand before the fat melts and the structure sets, which is why the oven starts cool.
* **Steam:** pricking the top lets steam escape, so the crust stays crisp instead of bursting or going soggy.
