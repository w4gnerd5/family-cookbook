# Flaky Pastry Folding Instructions - 2nd page

This manual method for laminating dough creates the classic "puff" effect through mechanical layering. By alternating rolling, folding, and chilling, you create dozens of alternating layers of dough and fat; during baking, the moisture in the fat turns to steam, forcing the layers apart to create a light, airy, and crisp texture.

## Ingredients
> *No ingredients are listed on this page; these instructions are for the final preparation of the dough.*

## Instructions
> 1. Place the dough in the refrigerator for 1 hour, or in the freezer for 15-20 minutes.
> 2. Take the dough out of the refrigerator.
> 3. Dust the dough with flour and roll it out into a strip approximately 5mm thick.
> 4. Fold the strip into four along its length: first, bring both ends into the center, and then fold the whole piece in half (as shown in the diagram). This step is essential for the dough to rise properly during baking.
> 5. Continue the process of rolling and folding. Roll it out into a strip again and fold it in the exact same manner.
> 6. Wrap the dough in plastic wrap and place it back in the refrigerator for 1 hour, or in the freezer for 15-20 minutes.
> 7. Repeat this entire cycle twice to ensure the finished pastry turns out light and flaky!

## Notes
Temperature control is the most critical factor for success. If the fat (butter) becomes too soft or melts into the flour, the layers will fuse and the pastry will not rise. If the dough starts to feel sticky or elastic while rolling, return it to the freezer immediately for 10 minutes before continuing.
