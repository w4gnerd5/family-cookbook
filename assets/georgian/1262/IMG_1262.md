# Easter Paska/Kulich

This traditional Georgian baking technique emphasizes the importance of double-rising and temperature control to ensure a light, airy texture, which is a hallmark of festive breads like *Paska*. The second proofing inside the mold and the final egg wash are critical steps for achieving the desired height and a glossy, golden-brown finish.

## Ingredients

* 1 L Milk
* 10 Eggs
* 1 kg Sugar
* 5 tsp Yeast (depending on the yeast)
* 800 g Melted butter
* ~ 2.5 kg Flour
* 400 g Raisins

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the Starter (Opara):** In a separate bowl, combine 1 L of warm milk with 2 tablespoons of sugar, flour, and yeast. Mixure should have the consistency of Oladiki dough. Wrap the bowl warmly and allow to rise. Once risen, add into the main mixure
2. **Main Mixture:** Melt the butter and incorporate it with sugar and gogli-mogli - eggs that have been whisked until pale and white.
3. **Combine:** Add spices (previously infused in cognac or spirits) to the butter and egg mixture.
4. **Dough Preparation:** Once the wet ingredients are combined, slowly begin adding the flour.
5. **Kneading:** The dough must be well formed and should not feel clumpy and sticky. If it starts to stick, grease your hands with oil and work it for a long time.
6. **Final Step:** Knead the raisins into the dough at the very end.
7. Place the dough in a warm spot. Once it has risen, knead it thoroughly and place it back in a warm area once again.
8. After the second rise, distribute the dough into baking molds so that it fills little less than a half of each mold.
9. Rest the dough in molds for a short while and let it rise.
10. Brush the top with egg yolk before baking
11. P.S. Before placing the dough into the molds, brush the molds with some oil and coat sides and the bottom with ground bread crumbs, so that Kulich doesn't stick to the mold

## Notes

* **Temperature Control:** The milk used for the yeast starter must be warm, never hot, or it will kill the yeast.
* **Dough Texture:** The Kulich dough should remain soft and "fluffy." Use oil on your hands rather than excessive flour if sticking occurs to maintain the correct consistency.
* **Spices:** For the best flavor, let your spices sit in cognac or alcohol before adding them to the dough.
* To ensure the best results, avoid applying an excessive amount of egg yolk to the top of the dough, as this can lead to burning.
