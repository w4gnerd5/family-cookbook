# Ancient Natufian Flatbread

This specimen represents a reconstruction of the world's oldest bread, based on charred remains found at the Natufian site of Shubayqa 1 in Jordan, dating back over 14,000 years. The core science behind this recipe is the processing of wild cereals and starchy tubers into fine flour; by removing tough husks and combining multiple carbohydrate sources, ancient humans created a energy-dense, portable dough that could be baked on hot stones long before the official start of agriculture.

## Ingredients:
> * Wild barley (*Hordeum spontaneum*)
> * Wild einkorn wheat (*Triticum monococcum*)
> * Wild emmer wheat (*Triticum dicoccum*)
> * Club-rush tubers (*Bolboschoenus glaucus*)
> * Water

## Instructions:
> 1. Grind the wild cereal grains and starchy tubers into a fine flour using stone mortars or grinding slabs.
> 2. Sieve the ground mixture to remove larger pieces of husk and grit.
> 3. Combine the fine flour with water, mixing thoroughly to form a thick, pliable dough.
> 4. Divide the dough and flatten it into thin, disc-like shapes.
> 5. Place the dough discs directly onto flat stones heated by a hearth fire or bury them in hot ashes to bake until firm and slightly charred.

## Notes:
> Ensure the tubers are thoroughly dried before grinding to achieve a fine flour consistency. Texture is key; the flatbread should be thin enough to cook quickly through the center without burning the exterior.
